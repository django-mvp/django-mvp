Documentation, settings and filtered lists all want the same thing: a narrow column beside the page that stays in view while the reader scrolls. Put a <c-mvp.page.side> in the page.side block and the page makes room for it.

When to use it

  • A table of contents for a long page, like the one beside this text.
  • Filters for a list, kept open while the results change underneath them.
  • Related links or a summary of the record a page is about.

Leave the block empty and nothing changes: the page is drawn exactly as it was, with no extra wrapper and no drawer.

Adding a side panel

Fill the block with one panel. Its label is the heading a reader sees and the name a screen reader announces.

{% verbatim %}{% block page.side %}
  <c-mvp.page.side label="On this page" width="sm" align="content">
    <ul class="menu menu-sm">…</ul>
  </c-mvp.page.side>
{% endblock page.side %}{% endverbatim %}

A page that does not extend page_view.html uses the layout component directly and passes the panel in its side slot.

Narrow screens

Below its breakpoint there is no room for a column, so the panel either becomes a drawer or is stacked with the content.

A drawer

The default. The panel is an off-canvas drawer that slides in from its own edge, and it closes on Escape, on the close button or on a tap outside it.

Stacked

With collapse="stack" the panel is drawn full width, under the title. A panel at the start comes before the content and a panel at the end after it, so the order matches the order a screen reader and the Tab key take. Suits a short panel the reader needs before the content, such as a picker.

Not shown

A drawer with nothing on the page to open it is simply not shown on a narrow screen. Suits a table of contents, which only repeats the headings the reader is already scrolling past. Untick Toggle above to see it.

Width, position and alignment

Three widths: sm for a list of headings, md for most filters, lg for a panel with wide controls such as date ranges. The panel sits after the content by default; position="start" puts it before.

align="title", the default, starts the column level with the page title. align="content" starts it under the title, level with the content, and gives the title the whole width.

The breakpoint defaults to xl. With the app sidebar open, a large screen leaves too little room for both the content and a second column, so the panel waits for an extra-large one.

Sticking under the header

The column sticks just under the app header, whatever height the header is: a tray, a wrapped breadcrumb trail or a project's own content all move it. When the panel is taller than the space left on screen it scrolls on its own, and the page scrolls with the wheel once it reaches the end.

The same measurement keeps a heading reached from a link, or a field reached with Tab, from landing under the header.

Accessibility

The panel is a complementary landmark named by its heading. It is a tab stop, so a reader who cannot use a pointer can scroll it with the keyboard when it is taller than the screen.
